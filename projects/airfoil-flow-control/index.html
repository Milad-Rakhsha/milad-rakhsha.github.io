Passive flow control with OpenFOAM

Master’s research · UMass Dartmouth · 2015

For my master’s thesis at the University of Massachusetts Dartmouth, I used OpenFOAM to study passive control of flow separation on the NACA 63-018 airfoil. The study compared vortex generators and leading-edge tubercles, motivated by hydrokinetic vertical-axis turbines whose blades can operate in stall at low tip-speed ratios.

Numerical setup

I constructed structured parametric meshes with blockMesh and used the steady-state simpleFoam solver with the k–ω SST turbulence model. The simulations covered angles of attack from 0° to 18° at Reynolds numbers of 5 × 105 and 3 × 106. The parameter study varied the geometry and placement of vortex generators, and the amplitude and wavelength of leading-edge tubercles.

Results

For the configurations studied, double-sided vortex generators increased the lift-to-drag ratio in stall and post-stall conditions. The optimal positions of the non-submerged vortex generators were 10% and 20% of the chord from the leading edge at the lower and higher Reynolds numbers, respectively. Leading-edge tubercles did not improve performance for the cases examined.

These findings are specific to the airfoil, flow conditions, turbulence model, and geometric parameter space of the study. They illustrate the need to evaluate passive flow-control devices across operating conditions rather than infer their benefit from geometry alone.
