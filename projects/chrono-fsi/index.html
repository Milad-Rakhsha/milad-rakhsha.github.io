Project Chrono: fluid–solid interaction

Project Chrono · SPH · Fluid–solid interaction

I was a developer of Project Chrono and one of the early developers of its fluid solver. During my PhD at UW–Madison, I worked on coupling particle-based fluid dynamics to rigid and flexible multibody systems. A large body of work in Chrono::FSI was based on that research.

Fluid–solid coupling

I developed a partitioned framework that connects an SPH fluid solver to Chrono’s multibody dynamics. Boundary-condition-enforcing markers attached to the solid communicate its motion to the fluid and transfer fluid forces back to the solid. This let me retain separate fluid and solid discretizations while accounting for their two-way interaction.

I validated the coupling on problems with both rigid and flexible bodies, including moving cylinders and elastic gates. For flexible structures, I needed the interface to track deformation as well as rigid motion.

Numerical methods and implementation

I worked on projection-based implicit SPH methods and the pressure and velocity systems they produce. I implemented particle operations and Krylov solvers on GPUs and used CPU parallelism for flexible multibody dynamics. I applied the framework to free-surface flows, viscous and non-Newtonian materials, and granular flow modeled as a continuum.

The SPH post gives a short overview of the numerical work; my dissertation contains the formulations, validation studies, and applications.
