Implicit SPH for coupled fluid and multibody dynamics

PhD research · UW–Madison · 2019

During my PhD at UW–Madison, I worked on implicit SPH methods for incompressible flow and coupled them to rigid and flexible body dynamics. I wanted to simulate systems in which fluid forces move or deform a structure, and that motion changes the flow in return.

Implicit fluid simulation

I developed and evaluated projection-based formulations that solve for pressure and velocity implicitly. The pressure solve enforces incompressibility, while implicit treatment of viscosity helps with the time-step restrictions encountered in viscous flows. I implemented particle operations and linear solvers on the GPU, including GMRES and BiCGStab, and studied the balance between solve cost, accuracy, and stability.

I compared several SPH formulations on standard flow benchmarks, then compared SPH with an Eulerian finite-element method for free-surface and fluid–solid interaction problems. I was interested in how the choice of discretization affected both the computed response and the cost of obtaining it.

Coupling fluids and solids in Chrono

I connected the fluid solver to rigid and flexible multibody dynamics through two-way force and motion exchange. Fluid forces drive the solid response, and boundary markers attached to the solid communicate its motion back to the fluid. A large body of work in Chrono::FSI was based on this research.

My dissertation, Computational Dynamics of Continuum and Discrete Systems Using Lagrangian Methods, brings this work together with my studies of granular flow and frictional multibody dynamics.
