Aerodynamics at Roblox

Roblox · Simulation Engineer · 2020–2022

I joined Roblox in 2020 as a simulation engineer. One of the projects I worked on was an efficient aerodynamic model that let developers build experiences with lift and drag acting on objects throughout the world.

Precomputing the aerodynamic response

The challenge was to make aerodynamic forces inexpensive enough to use across large game worlds, including objects with complex meshes. I moved the geometry-dependent work out of the simulation loop: before an experience loaded, we precomputed an approximate directional aerodynamic response for each body and stored it in a compact representation.

At runtime, I used a cubemap-like lookup to approximate the response for the current motion, rather than revisit the full mesh at every step. This kept the CPU cost low while allowing aerodynamic forces to respond to an object’s shape and orientation. In the large experiences I worked on, we could keep aerodynamics active on world objects with a CPU cost of roughly a millisecond per update.

For the public feature announcement and examples, see Introducing Aerodynamic Forces — Studio Beta on the Roblox Developer Forum.

Working at Roblox

I really enjoyed my time at Roblox. I worked with very smart people and learned a great deal from them about building simulation systems under the constraints of a live platform. It was especially rewarding to see developers turn the aerodynamic behavior into new kinds of gameplay.

I stayed at Roblox until 2022, when I joined NVIDIA’s Isaac Gym team.
