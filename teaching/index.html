Teaching and notes

An Introduction to Computational Fluid Dynamics

University of Wisconsin–Madison · November 2019
Milad Rakhsha and Dan Negrut

I taught a few introductory lectures on computational fluid dynamics as part of ME 751: Advanced Computational Multibody Dynamics at UW–Madison in 2019. In these lectures, I connected the continuum equations of fluid mechanics with their numerical discretization. The notes and accompanying slides are available below. The notes develop the Eulerian and Lagrangian descriptions, derive mass and momentum conservation, and introduce the differential operators used in the governing equations.

The numerical-methods sections cover finite differences, finite volumes, Galerkin finite elements, and smoothed particle hydrodynamics. Topics include conservative discretization, advective and diffusive fluxes, boundary-condition enforcement, numerical quadrature, and the construction of discrete systems.

The final section introduces Helmholtz decomposition and incremental and non-incremental projection methods for incompressible flow, connecting the pressure solve to the divergence constraint on velocity.
